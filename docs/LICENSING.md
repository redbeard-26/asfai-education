The repository's [LICENSE](../LICENSE) applies only to material for which the ASFAI Education contributors have the right to grant that license.

The Apache license does not relicense imported curricula, standards, taxonomies, assessment items, media, or learner artifacts. Each source retains its own terms. Examples relevant to the design include:

## Data-package boundary

Code, original schemas, and original documentation can remain Apache-2.0 while datasets are distributed as separately identified packages under their applicable licenses. Each dataset package should include:

- a machine-readable manifest;
- the exact source and retrieval date;
- the dataset license and required notices;
- checksums for source and transformed records;
- a transformation log;
- a clear statement of which fields were authored by ASFAI.

Do not place third-party data in an Apache-only directory or describe the entire repository as uniformly Apache-licensed if differently licensed data is later added.

## Contributions

Unless a contribution states otherwise and is accepted on that basis, contributions are made under the same Apache-2.0 terms as the repository. Contributors must have the right to submit their work. They should not submit copied curriculum text, proprietary assessment items, student work, or personal data merely because it is technically accessible.

## Student and user content

A software license is not a privacy policy or terms-of-service agreement. Student work, conversations, evidence, and records require separate product terms, consent and authority analysis, retention rules, access controls, and deletion/export procedures. They should never be treated as open-source repository content.

## Names and marks

Apache-2.0 does not grant rights to ASFAI names, logos, or third-party trademarks except as required for reasonable descriptive use. A separate trademark policy may be added if the project develops formal branding.

This policy is operational guidance and not legal advice. Obtain qualified review before distributing third-party standards data or deploying a student-record system.
